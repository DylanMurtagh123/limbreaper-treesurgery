What is crown lifting?

Same tree, more
room underneath

Crown lifting removes a tree's lowest branches to raise the height of the canopy above the ground. Unlike a reduction, it doesn't touch the top or sides of the tree at all — the overall height and shape stay exactly as they were. All that changes is the clearance underneath.

It's the go-to fix when a tree's lower growth has started catching delivery vans, brushing a roof, blocking a window, or crowding a path or driveway. Rather than removing the tree or drastically reshaping it, lifting solves the specific problem while leaving everything else alone.

We always aim to keep a sensible proportion of live crown above the lift — taking too much off too low can leave a tree looking top-heavy, so we'll recommend a height that actually solves the clearance issue without overdoing it.

Faq

Crown lifting,
questions answered

The things people usually want to know before booking a lift.

What is crown lifting?

Crown lifting is the removal of a tree's lowest branches to raise the height of the canopy above the ground. It's used to create clearance underneath — over a driveway, path, road or roofline — without reducing the tree's overall height or changing its natural shape above that point.

How is crown lifting different from a crown reduction?

A crown reduction cuts back the top and sides of the canopy to make the whole tree smaller. Crown lifting only removes lower branches to raise clearance underneath — the top of the tree and its overall height stay exactly as they were.

How high can you lift a crown?

It depends on the tree's size and how it's built, but we always aim to keep a sensible proportion of live crown above the lift — taking off too much too low can leave a tree looking top-heavy and put unnecessary stress on it. We'll recommend a height that solves your clearance problem without overdoing it.

Will crown lifting harm the tree?

Done properly and in moderation, no. We prune back to the trunk or a suitable junction rather than leaving stubs, and we won't recommend removing more lower growth than the tree can comfortably lose in one go.

Do I need permission for crown lifting?

Sometimes. Trees within a conservation area, or covered by a Tree Preservation Order (TPO), usually need council permission before any work goes ahead, crown lifting included. We check this for you as part of the free site visit.

What's a typical reason people need crown lifting?

Low branches that catch delivery vans or bins on a driveway, overhang a pavement or road, block a window or knock against a roof are the most common reasons we're called out for a lift. It's also often done alongside a hedge or garden tidy-up to let more light into a lawn or border.
