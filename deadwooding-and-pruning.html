What is dead-wooding?

Taking out what's
already gone

Dead-wooding is exactly what it sounds like — removing dead, dying or damaged branches from an otherwise healthy tree. It's not about reshaping the tree or changing how it looks; it's about taking out the specific limbs that have stopped growing and could fail without warning.

Unlike a living branch, a dead one doesn't bend, flex or give any sign it's about to come down — no creaking, no leaning, nothing to see coming. Over a garden, driveway, path, or anywhere people or cars pass underneath, that.s a serious risk worth dealing with.

We only remove what actually needs to come out. Some deadwood is completely normal, even valuable for wildlife, and we'll always explain what we're taking and why rather than stripping a tree back further than it needs.

What to look for

Signs it's time
for a check

You don't need to be an expert to spot the early signs. If any of these apply, it's worth getting a tree looked at:

  • Bare, brittle branches with no leaves while the rest of the tree's in leaf

  • Bark that's peeling, cracked or missing from a limb

  • Fungal growth on the trunk or major limbs

  • A branch overhanging somewhere people regularly walk, sit or park

  • It's simply been a few years since the tree was last checked over

Faq

Dead-wooding,
questions answered

The things people usually want to know before booking a dead-wood check.

What exactly is dead-wooding?

Dead-wooding is the removal of dead, dying or damaged branches from an otherwise healthy tree. It's not about reshaping the tree — it's about taking out the specific limbs that have stopped growing and could fail or drop without warning.

Why can't dead branches just be left alone?

A dead branch doesn't bend or give the way a living one does, and it can fail with no warning — no creaking, no leaning, nothing to see coming. Over a garden, driveway, path or anywhere people or cars pass underneath, that.s a serious risk worth removing before it becomes a problem.

How is dead-wooding different from a crown reduction?

A crown reduction reshapes a tree by cutting back live growth to reduce its overall size. Dead-wooding only removes what's already dead or damaged — the living parts of the tree are left untouched. Many jobs involve a bit of both.

Does a tree with a lot of deadwood need to come down?

Not usually. Some deadwood is completely normal, especially in mature trees, and even provides valuable habitat for insects and birds where it's not a hazard. We only recommend removing what's actually a risk to people or property, and we'll always explain our reasoning rather than just cutting everything back.

How often should a tree be checked for deadwood?

For a tree near a house, driveway or somewhere people regularly are, a check every couple of years is sensible — more often for older or already-declining trees. If you've noticed a specific branch that looks dead, it's worth getting it looked at sooner rather than waiting.

Do I need permission to remove deadwood?

Sometimes. Trees within a conservation area, or covered by a Tree Preservation Order (TPO), can still require council permission even for deadwood removal. We check this for you as part of the free site visit.
